A chaser has one job: overwrite the alcohol before your brain files it as "bad." The best chaser is the one that hits fast, tastes strong, and contrasts the liquor instead of matching it.

Here's the cheat sheet, then the reasoning.

## The chart

| Liquor | Best chaser | Also good |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whiskey / bourbon | Ginger ale | Cola, pickle juice, iced tea |
| Tequila | Lime + salt | Orange juice, grapefruit soda, Squirt |
| Vodka | Cranberry juice | Lemonade, orange juice, plain water |
| Rum (white) | Pineapple juice | Orange juice, coconut water |
| Rum (spiced/dark) | Cola | Apple juice, root beer |
| Gin | Tonic water | Grapefruit juice, lemon-lime soda |
| Fireball / flavored whiskey | Cold apple juice | Cream soda |
| Jägermeister | Red Bull (the classic) | Orange juice |

## Why contrast beats matching

People instinctively chase sweet liquor with something sweet. That's backwards. If the chaser tastes *similar* to the shot, your palate blends them and you still get the alcohol note. A sharp contrast — sour against a sweet liqueur, ginger against whiskey's oak — gives your brain a completely different signal to latch onto.

Sour and spicy work best because they trigger a physical response (that little wince, the tingle) that distracts from taste entirely. That's why lime-and-salt tequila works and why pickle-juice whiskey shots — the "pickleback" — have a cult following.

## Timing matters more than the chaser

The single biggest mistake: reaching for the chaser *after* the burn has set in. You have about one second between swallowing the shot and the alcohol registering on your tongue. The chaser needs to be already in your hand, already open, ideally already at your lips.

If you find yourself fumbling for the chaser every time, that's the exact problem a [shot straw](/) solves — the shot and the chaser go down together, so there's no window to miss.

## Chasers that don't work

- **Anything warm.** Warm soda, room-temperature juice — heat amplifies alcohol.
- **Beer.** It's a slow sipper, not a chaser. You'll still taste the shot.
- **Milk / cream.** Coats your tongue but does nothing for the vapor you breathe in.
- **Water, mostly.** Fine for clean vodka, useless for anything harsh. It dilutes but doesn't overwrite.

## The short version

Chase with something cold, sharp, and already in your hand. Contrast the liquor, don't match it. And if the timing keeps beating you, take the fumbling out of the equation.
